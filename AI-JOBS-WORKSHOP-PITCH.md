# AI is disrupting entry-level jobs. Is the job market actually fu**ed?

There's a lot of hubbub about AI disrupting the job market and taking everyone's jobs. What has been supremely surprising to me is just how few people actually understand how our economic system works.

Your job and your compensation inside of an organization are a direct function of the value you provide, at a price point the market decides. People seem to be very shocked by this: that it is not a moral or ethical judgment, and that their myriad of other random skills do not contribute to their compensation or their ability to secure a job.

This is important because AI is actively disrupting — not taking, disrupting — the human value proposition in our current market economics.

Here's the clear-eyed reality from the Claude Ambassador who lives and breathes and builds in this technology all day, every single day: the models now are really good. They easily let me, an individual, 10x my productivity while maintaining the same workload. And you only get 10x productivity by actively removing the opportunity for, and simultaneously devaluing, the work a human could perform in that same domain, right?

If you're a student or a job seeker, you're feeling this first. The real data shows it: Stanford researchers found a 13% relative drop in employment for 22- to 25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed jobs, while older workers in those same jobs held steady or grew. So you should really take it upon yourself to learn what areas AI is actively disrupting and where the human is still incredibly valuable.

Because this is not a story of AI taking everyone's jobs. This is a story of where the role of the human becomes significantly more valuable. And in order for you to secure your position as a valuable person in this ecosystem, you have to understand exactly, from a technological perspective, where AI fails in a business context and who bears that risk.

Here's one real example. Harvard and BCG researchers gave consultants a problem AI couldn't solve. On their own, they got it right 84% of the time. Using AI, only 60 to 70% of the time. That's AI falling down in a business context, and the human is the one who bears that risk.

Two, we will show you what skills are needed in today's market, and how to adapt your current skill set to be market competitive.

Three, I will give you real live examples, from a technology perspective, of where AI falls down.
